Business Domain Model

A business domain model is a structured diagram of the real-world concepts in a business - things, their properties, and how they relate. It captures nouns (e.g., Customer, Order, Invoice), key attributes (e.g., Order:total), and relationships (e.g., Customer places Order) using the business's own language. It's technology-agnostic that focuses on business concepts, not implementation.

A domain model is not a database schema or data model; it is a conceptual representation of the business domain. In contrast a database model is storage-oriented (tables, indexes). One domain concept can map to many database tables or vice versa. Likewise, a domain model is not a technical specification or a detailed design document. Rather it helps align stakeholders, developers, and analysts on the core concepts and relationships in the business. The domain model serves as a foundation for communication, analysis, and design throughout the API development lifecycle.

Creating a business domain model involves collaboration between business stakeholders, domain experts, and developers. The process includes:

  • Eliciting business language: Collect real terms from domain experts; avoid system jargon
  • Identifying key business concepts and entities
  • Defining attributes and properties of each entity
  • Establishing relationships between entities
  • Iteratively refining the model based on feedback and new insights
For example, the following diagram illustrates a simple business domain model for an insurance system. Note that the diagram has been simplified for brevity. In practice, a business domain model typically includes many more entities, attributes, and relationships. The diagram illustrates the following key business concepts:
  • Entities: Provider, Insured, Insurance Plan, Benefit Set, Service
  • Subtypes: Hospital, Physician, and ThirdParty entities are types of Providers; Subscriber and Dependent entities are types of Insured
  • Attributes: An Insurance Plan has name, deductibleInNetwork, and deductibleOutOfNetwork attributes; Insured has nameFirst, nameLast, phone, and email attributes
  • Relationships: A Provider offers one or more InsurancePlans; Insured is enrolled in one or more InsurancePlans
  • Inheritance: Subscriber and Dependent entities inherit common attributes and relationships from the Insured entity (indicated by the clear arrowhead)
  • Multiplicity: An InsurancePlan has one or more BenefitSets; a BenefitSet includes one or more Services
  • Composition: Deleting an InsurancePlan also deletes the associated BenefitSets (indicated by the black arrowhead). Likewise, deleting a BenefitSet also deletes the associated Services
